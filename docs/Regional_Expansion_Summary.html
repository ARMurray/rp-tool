What the crosswalk work looks like

737

Pollutant names to review

The name matches that were not exact: 335 high, 208 medium and 194 low confidence. A name maps to the same ICIS code in every state, so each is reviewed once for the whole country.

~1,300

Criteria that are equations, not numbers

The national database says "See equation" or "See table" for hardness metals, ammonia and similar criteria. Each state's equation has to be built by hand, as it was for Puerto Rico. Expect roughly 10–15 distinct equation families.

1,302

Distinct water class labels

Median of 21 classes per state, 83 in Minnesota. Puerto Rico has 4. The class picker, seven metal IDs and the ammonia ID are hardcoded to Puerto Rico and need to read from each state's list.

Defaults we need from each region

These are the assumptions built into the tool today. For each one: what does your region use, and who decides — the region, the state or the permit writer?

  • Dilution ratioToday: 1, no mixing zone. Which design flow (7Q10, 1Q10, 30Q10, harmonic mean) and what share of stream flow?
  • Acute vs chronic vs human healthToday: one dilution ratio and the daily maximum for all. Do these need separate treatment?
  • Confidence level and percentileToday: 95% and 95%. Do you use 99% and 99%?
  • Small data setsToday: CV of 0.6 when fewer than 10 samples. Is there a minimum number of samples?
  • Non-detects and no-discharge codesZero, half the detection limit, or the limit? Are no-discharge periods counted as zero?
  • Background concentrationToday: ignored. Do you require an upstream value in the mass balance?
  • HardnessToday: 100 mg/L. What do your states specify, and with what floor and cap?
  • Ammonia inputsToday: pH 6.0 and 30 °C, specific to Puerto Rico. Seasonal values? Mussels present?
  • Narrative standardsOil and grease and surfactants run as detection tests today. What is your rule?
  • Data windowToday: 5 years in the app. What matches your permit cycle?
